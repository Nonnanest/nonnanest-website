How to choose a baby monitor

A framework for the questions that actually matter.

There are a lot of baby monitors, and a lot of opinions about them. This is a short, honest guide to help you find the right one for your family. No rankings. No pressure.

By Nathan Overbey, founder Updated September 14, 2026 6 min read

The short answer

To choose a baby monitor, work through five questions in order:

  1. Do you want audio-visual only, or audio-visual plus wellness insights?
  2. If you want wellness insights, which signal would you actually act on?
  3. Should it sit on your baby's body, or read from a distance?
  4. WiFi, or no WiFi?
  5. What does it cost after you buy it?
Start here

Almost every parent shopping today starts from the same place: an audio-visual monitor. A camera, a screen, sound. That part is a given. The real choice is whether to add a second layer on top.

The framework

Five questions, in order

Work through these and you'll land on the right kind of monitor without needing anyone's top-ten list. Tap any question to open it.

A great camera and clear audio is genuinely all some families need. You can see your baby, hear your baby, done. Plenty of happy parents stop right here.

Others want a little more: a steady signal overnight about how their baby is doing. That extra layer is the newer part of the category, and it's where monitors start to differ a lot. If you want it, the next question is the one that matters.

"Wellness monitor" isn't one thing. Different devices read different signals about your baby's body, and no single one reads all of them. More isn't automatically better. The right signal is the one you'd actually act on.

Tap the one that sounds most like what you're after:

  • Breathing and movement devices are the most watchful, and the most likely to wake you. A fair choice if that specific reassurance is what you're after.

  • Heart rate and oxygen is the most data-rich flavor. It's also the most clinical-feeling, and worth asking yourself whether you'll act on a stream of numbers or just watch them.

  • Skin temperature awareness sits at the calm end of the spectrum. A gentle comfort signal, not a stream of vitals, and not a medical measurement. This is the lane Nonnanest was built for. How skin-temperature awareness works

Wellness insights get read one of two ways. Wearables (a sock, a clip) sit on your baby and can read more, at the cost of something on your newborn all night. Contactless devices read from a distance, with nothing on your baby at all.

If a strap on a newborn gives you pause, contactless is worth a look. If the extra data a wearable provides is what you're after, that's a fair reason to choose one.
Wearable vs. contactless monitors, in more depth

A WiFi monitor lets you check in from anywhere through an app. Genuinely useful for some families. The trade-off is that the monitor lives on your home network, and often a company's servers.

A non-WiFi monitor stays offline and connects straight to its own screen: nothing to hack, nothing in the cloud. Neither is "better." Now you know the trade-off.
Do baby monitors need WiFi?

The one parents miss. Some monitors carry a subscription, often over a hundred dollars a year, to unlock features or history. A one-time-purchase monitor doesn't.

Before you buy, find the ongoing cost. It changes the real price a lot.

Worth knowing

A quick word on "AI" monitors

You'll see monitors advertise cry detection, sound differentiation, motion and rollover alerts, even face-covering alerts. These are real and can be genuinely helpful. It's worth knowing what they are: they make the camera and audio smarter. They don't read your baby's body.

So they sit on the audio-visual side of the choice, not the wellness-insight side. A useful layer to want, just a different one. And a fair thing to ask any of them: how often does it cry wolf? A note from the parent who built one

I'll be honest about my bias. I built a baby monitor, so I'm not a neutral party. But I built it because I went through the exact research you're doing now, and nothing fit what I wanted.

I wanted the camera and audio, of course. But I also wanted a gentle wellness signal overnight, without a strap on my kids and without our nursery being one more thing on the internet. I wanted skin temperature awareness, contactless, no WiFi, no subscription. I couldn't find all of it in one place, so I made it. My own kids were the first ones it was ever on, before I sold a single unit to anyone else.

You might weigh these questions differently than I did, and that's the whole point of this guide. Use the five questions. Pick the monitor that fits your family. If that turns out to be mine, I'd be glad to have you.
